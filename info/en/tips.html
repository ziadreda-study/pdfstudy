Tips that make your studying smarter

Studying more is not the point - studying right is. Here is how to get the most out of your files and the app.

1. Read once with no pen

Skim the whole file quickly before you highlight anything. The goal is to learn the shape of the lecture: where the introduction is, where the definitions are, and where the examples are. People who highlight from the first line end up highlighting half the file without knowing what really matters.

2. Organize your highlight colors

  • One color for definitions and terms.
  • One color for examples and practical cases.
  • One color for what the lecturer emphasized.

When you come back before the exam, your eyes follow the colors, not the words, and revision takes a quarter of the time.

3. Write in the margins yourself

Do not stop at highlighting. Use the text or pen tool and write a summary of each idea in your own words next to it. Putting the idea in your own words is the strongest way to fix it in your memory.

4. Test yourself instead of rereading

The strongest study method is "active recall": close the file and ask yourself "what was on this page?" Try to answer from memory, then open it and check. Rereading makes you feel you understand when you are really just memorizing the look of the page.

5. Review at spaced intervals

Instead of studying a lecture once for a long time, review it quickly after a day, after a week, and before the exam. The file copy you saved with your work is perfect for this because it holds only what matters.

6. Split your time

Study for 25 minutes with full focus, then take a 5-minute break, and repeat. After 4 rounds take a longer break. Keep the phone away or in airplane mode during the 25 minutes.

7. Translation is not memorizing

When you translate an English term in the app, do not just memorize the translation. Understand the meaning, connect it to the example in the lecture, and write your understanding next to it on the page.

You can apply all these tips right inside the app: colored highlights, writing in the margins, and saving a revision copy.
